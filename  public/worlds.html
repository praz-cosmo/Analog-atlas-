01 / OUR HOME

Earth

Earth is the reference environment for analog research. Field teams can collect samples, test instruments, map geology and rehearse mission procedures here.

What Earth helps us investigate

  • Volcanic processes and basaltic terrain
  • Extreme dryness and preservation of possible biosignatures
  • Ice, glaciers, salts and subsurface structures
  • Rover navigation and remote field operations

02 / OUR NEAREST NEIGHBOR

The Moon

The Moon preserves a record of impacts and volcanic history. Its surface also presents challenges for future exploration, including radiation exposure, abrasive dust and permanently shadowed polar regions.

Questions researchers investigate

  • Could lava tubes offer natural shelter?
  • How can instruments detect buried ice?
  • How do basaltic terrains form and change?
  • What can terrestrial impact structures teach us?

03 / THE RED PLANET

Mars

Mars preserves landscapes shaped by volcanism, wind, impacts and ancient water. Robotic missions investigate its geology and whether some past environments could have supported microbial life.

Questions researchers investigate

  • How can rovers navigate rugged terrain?
  • How do salts and minerals record environmental history?
  • How might biosignatures be preserved in rocks?
  • How do wind-shaped surface patterns evolve?
